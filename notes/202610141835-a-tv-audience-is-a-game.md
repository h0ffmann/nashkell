# A TV audience is a game

I once had to estimate a television channel's audience, as a contractor. The plain estimate
reads the channel's own past: what its programmes drew on channel Y, Z, K, and so on. But an
audience at a given hour is shared. What one channel holds, the others do not. So the better
measure of a channel's power is its competition.

Written as a game: the players are the channels; a strategy is what a channel airs at each hour;
the payoff is the share of the audience it holds, which depends on what every other channel
chose. A forecast from the channel's own history alone treats the rivals as fixed. A forecast
that models their choices treats the audience as the outcome of a game.

The game theory came later, only at the end. The idea had its shape before it had its name. Now
I am fairly sure that I think in these terms.

**Open question.** No payoff was measured and no model was fitted. Whether modelling the rivals
beats a channel's own history as an estimate is not known here.
